## 2. Laws, Permits, and the “Ban‑with‑Exceptions” Model  

New Jersey treats big cats as **non‑domestic carnivores** over 25 lb, subject to the **Animal Welfare Act (N.J.S.A. 4:22‑1 et seq.)**. The key points for anyone curious (or concerned) about private ownership:

1. **Special Permit Required** – Issued by the **Division of Fish & Wildlife** after a site inspection and a 30‑day public comment period.  
2. **2018 “Big‑Cat Ban”** – Replaced the old “permit‑only” system with a **ban‑with‑exceptions** framework. Only **licensed zoological facilities** (zoos, sanctuaries, accredited breeding programs) may keep lions.  
3. **2021 Amendment** – Mandates **annual health‑checks** and **record‑keeping** for all permit holders.  
4. **Enforcement Example** – The 2022 “lion escape” in Somerset County (a 3‑month‑old cub slipped out of a private farm) triggered a statewide review of permit compliance and reinforced the ban’s importance for public safety.

These regulations have shifted the state’s focus from **curiosity collections** to **public‑safety and animal‑welfare** priorities, ensuring that any lion you see in NJ is under professional care.
